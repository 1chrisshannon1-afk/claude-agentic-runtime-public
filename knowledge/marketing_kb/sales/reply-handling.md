# Reply Handling

What to do when a person answers outreach (an email campaign, a referral-partner note, a reactivation
message) or a new inquiry lands outside working hours: the step between "a reply exists" and "the person
is qualified and booked". Response-time research for inbound inquiries is in `speed-to-lead.md`;
follow-up touch counts and channel limits are in `follow-up-cadences.md`; qualification is in
`lead-qualification.md`; what to say first in a campaign is in `outreach-levers-and-drafting.md`. This
file does not repeat them.

**Evidence grade.** The practices below are distilled from one practitioner interview (published
2026-10-09, retrieved 2026-10-11), a single self-reported source from inside a tool-vendor ecosystem.
What it asserts about timing or effect is tagged `UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation`. Rules this
corpus adopts are tagged `(internal doctrine)`. The speaker and the interview's title are not named in this corpus; the grade rests on the source type, not on who said it. [KB-SALES-106]

## 1. Set a reply target before the first send

**[KB-SALES-096] (internal doctrine)** Before launch, decide who answers replies, by when, and who covers
when they cannot. A campaign with no named reply owner wastes its best leads.

**[KB-SALES-097] UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation** The source says that for startup and
small-business audiences many replies start arriving around 8 or 9 pm, so a next-morning answer can be many
hours late (about 13 in its example); it presents this as depending on the audience.
**(internal doctrine)** Measure the business's own reply-arrival times from its first sends and set the
target from that, not from the source.

**[KB-SALES-098] (internal doctrine)** Speed comes from preparation (a draft ready for the common
answers), not from skipping approval.

**[KB-SALES-107] (internal doctrine)** Route replies from every channel and every sending mailbox into one
place that the named reply owner reads: a shared inbox or label, a forwarding rule, or one view in a tool the
business already uses. More than one sending mailbox, or a second channel such as direct messages on a social
network, scatters replies, and the reply target in [KB-SALES-096] is only as fast as the least-watched place.
Where a network forbids automated access, the person reads it by hand and logs the reply in the same queue;
no outside tool logs in to it. A shared queue only collects replies: it does not answer, book or send by
itself ([KB-SALES-101]). `UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation` The source recommends a tool that gathers email and network-sequencer
replies into one inbox and adds an AI agent that books meetings.

## 2. Build a reply knowledge base once

**[KB-SALES-099] (internal doctrine)** Before launch, write down (dictation is fine) what a good reply draws
on: the common objections with an honest answer to each; how the service works, what is included and what
is not; pricing guidance the owner has approved, never invented by the assistant; real, documented results
the business may name; how to book and who takes a reply that needs a person.

**[KB-SALES-100] (internal doctrine)** Date it, assign an owner, and review it after the first sends and
whenever an answer changes. It contains no personal health information or customer records.

## 3. Draft for approval

**[KB-SALES-101] (internal doctrine)** An assistant drafts each reply from the knowledge base; a person
approves and sends. A reply that commits money, a date, a discount, a guarantee or a legal position always
goes to a person. Any automatic send is limited to what the business's approval settings already allow
(for example a plain acknowledgment that states the next step honestly). A seat whose own rules forbid
sending never sends automatically, whatever the settings allow.

**[KB-SALES-102] (internal doctrine)** A reply that asks to stop, unsubscribe or complains is handled as an
opt-out within one business day and suppressed everywhere (see `follow-up-cadences.md` and
`../compliance/can-spam.md`); no further drafts
are produced for that person. Anything angry, legal or about safety goes to a person, not a draft.

## 4. Phone

**[KB-SALES-103] (internal doctrine)** If a reply includes a phone number, the default is a prompt written
reply offering times to talk. A person may phone someone who has asked to talk, within the call rules in
`follow-up-cadences.md` and the compliance corpus's TCPA file. Calls are never automated, prerecorded or
AI-voiced when the call is sales or outreach to a lead or prospect; any other automated or AI-voiced call
follows the compliance corpus's TCPA consent rules. A business whose own rules forbid
cold calling does not treat a reply as permission.

## Anti-patterns

- No reply owner, so replies sit until someone happens to look.
- Replies scattered across mailboxes and networks with no single queue the reply owner reads.
- An assistant that sends, books or commits on its own beyond the approval it is configured for.
- A knowledge base the assistant invented or nobody reviewed.
- Continuing to draft to someone who has opted out.
- Using the source's timing figures as targets instead of measuring the business's own.
