## Hashtags — the 2025–2026 change that makes older doctrine wrong

**Current mechanical limit: Instagram caps hashtags at 5 per post/Reel**, changed from the
historical 30-hashtag technical maximum [KB-SOCIAL-022] (third-party, multiple 2026
Instagram-hashtag-strategy explainers reporting a December 2025 platform-enforced cap, retrieved
2026-09-27 — **UNVERIFIED (primary source unreachable): no Instagram Help Center page documenting
this specific cap change was independently located and fetched during this pass.** Before this
doctrine is used to justify a specific hashtag count in a live campaign, an agent must locate and
cite Instagram's own current Help Center hashtag-limit page, since the mechanical cap is exactly
the kind of platform detail that changes without a press release.

**What this replaces:** older doctrine (including anything ported from a different vertical's own
corpus, e.g. a dental-practice corpus) that recommends 15–30 hashtags per post is stale advice if the
5-hashtag cap above is confirmed current. **Never port a hashtag-count recommendation from another corpus without
re-verifying the current cap first** — this is exactly the kind of fast-moving platform-mechanics
fact `shared/citation-style.md` flags as needing re-verification before it anchors a new
recommendation.

**Selection doctrine (mechanism, not count-dependent):** choose hashtags that describe what the
specific post is about and who it's for — a mix of a broad service-category tag, a
narrower/specific-project tag, and a local-geography tag — rather than maximizing volume. This
mechanism-level guidance is consistent across the platform's own historical hashtag guidance
regardless of the exact numeric cap in force at any given time.

## Stories, Reels, and Guides — what's actually available today

- **Stories**: 24-hour ephemeral content; supports interactive stickers (poll, question, quiz,
  countdown, link). The question/poll sticker is a legitimate way to earn the reply/comment signal
  named in `meta-organic-formats-and-ranking.md` — e.g. "Which finish do you like better, matte or
  gloss?" on a real in-progress job photo.
- **Reels**: the discovery-oriented format (see ranking doctrine). For a contractor, a Reel is the
  natural home for a time-lapse or before/after sequence of a real completed job.
- **Guides**: Instagram's "Guides" feature (curated collections of posts/products/places) has had
  reduced platform investment and visibility since its 2021 launch; **do not build a content
  strategy around Guides** without first re-verifying the feature still exists and is
  discoverable in the current app — UNVERIFIED (primary source unreachable: no current Instagram
  Help Center confirmation of Guides' status was checked during this pass; flagging this
  specifically because a stale corpus is exactly where a dead-feature recommendation would survive
  unnoticed).

## Cross-vertical porting: any number needs re-derivation

This file is written for a home-services vertical; a company pack for a different vertical (e.g. a
dental practice) uses its own vertical-specific numbers here, not these. Any specific
engagement-rate benchmark, follower-growth-rate target, or "best time to post" claim inherited from
one vertical's own social strategy and applied to another (in either direction — dental-to-home-
services or home-services-to-dental) is vertical-specific by construction: dental patients and
residential remodeling homeowners have different search/discovery behavior, purchase cycles, and
Instagram usage patterns. Port the **mechanism** only (e.g. "before/after visual proof performs well
for a service business selling a visible physical outcome" — true for both dental and remodeling)
and mark any specific **number** `UNVERIFIED — seeded from [source vertical] analog, needs [target
vertical] derivation` per `shared/citation-style.md` and `seo/INDEX.md`'s house-style rule.

## Contractor bend

The owner-operator running this account (see `shared/contractor-buyer-psychology.md` for the
B2B-software-buyer shape of this same archetype, when the company being served sells software
*to* businesses like this one) is phone-first and time-poor. The
practical Instagram doctrine for this business's own account: post real job photos taken on the phone already in the truck — no
studio setup, no separate content-production workflow — because platform ranking rewards real
engagement signals (comments, shares, watch-through) that a genuine, un-styled before/after photo
earns more reliably from a local audience than a polished stock-feeling post does. Never propose a
workflow that requires this buyer to learn a scheduling tool, a caption-writing framework, or a
hashtag research tool before they can post at all — the bar is "snap the photo you already took for
the customer anyway, add one sentence, post it."
