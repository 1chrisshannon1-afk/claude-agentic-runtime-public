# Meta organic formats and ranking — Facebook + Instagram Feed and Reels

What actually decides whether an organic Facebook/Instagram post reaches anyone, and which format
to use for which goal. Read this before recommending a posting cadence, a format mix, or telling
the business owner "the algorithm favors X" without a citation.

## The four Feed ranking signal groups

Instagram's own engineering/product team names four signal groups that decide Feed ranking, in
order of importance:

> "Your activity... Information about the post... Information about the person who posted...
> Your history of interacting with someone." [KB-SOCIAL-001] (official, Instagram/About Instagram
> blog, "Instagram Ranking Explained," published 2023-05-31, retrieved 2026-09-27:
> https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)

Instagram states it makes roughly a dozen behavioral predictions per piece of content, and names
five key Feed interactions it weighs most: time spent, comments, likes, shares, and profile taps
[KB-SOCIAL-002] (official, same source, retrieved 2026-09-27).

**What this means mechanically:** ranking is not a single "engagement rate" number. A post that
gets a comment and a share from someone the account interacts with often will outrank a post with
more raw likes from strangers. This is why "get comments, not just likes" is a real, sourced
recommendation, not a folk claim — comments and shares are named predictions; likes are one of
several, not the only signal.

## The four Reels ranking signal groups

Reels ranks on a related but distinct set of four groups, also in order:

> "Your activity... Your history of interacting with the person who posted... Information about
> the reel... Information about the person who posted." [KB-SOCIAL-003] (official, same Instagram
> blog post, retrieved 2026-09-27)

Key Reels predictions: likelihood to reshare, likelihood to watch all the way through, likelihood
to like, and likelihood to visit the audio page [KB-SOCIAL-004] (official, same source, retrieved
2026-09-27).

**Content-quality demotions — named explicitly, not inferred:**

> Instagram aims to reduce visibility for "low-resolution or watermarked reels, reels that are
> muted or contain borders, reels that are majority text, or reels that have already been posted
> on Instagram." [KB-SOCIAL-005] (official, same source, retrieved 2026-09-27)

**Operational read for a home-services company's content:** a Reel re-exported from another platform still
carrying that platform's watermark (a TikTok logo baked into the frame, a border from a
third-party editor) is demoted by name in Instagram's own doctrine — never post a Reel with a
visible cross-platform watermark; re-export or re-record natively instead.

## Watch time, sends-per-reach, likes-per-reach — third-party-reported, not independently
## re-verified here

Multiple third-party sources report that Instagram leadership has named watch time, "sends per
reach" (DM shares divided by reach), and "likes per reach" as the most heavily weighted
cross-surface ranking signals in 2025–2026 commentary [KB-SOCIAL-006] (third-party, aggregated
from multiple 2026 Instagram-algorithm explainer posts citing Instagram head Adam Mosseri's public
statements, retrieved 2026-09-27 — no single primary Meta document was independently located
during this pass naming these three signals together in that priority order; treat the specific
ranking — watch time first, sends-per-reach second — as **UNVERIFIED (primary source
unreachable/not located)** until an agent locates and re-cites the original Mosseri statement or
an official Meta post making the same claim). What both the official Instagram blog post above
and this third-party reporting agree on, independently: shares/sends and full watch-throughs are
weighted more heavily than raw likes. That agreement is the load-bearing fact; the exact relative
ranking of the three is not.

## Format decision table

| Goal | Format | Why (cite) |
|---|---|---|
| Reach new, non-following accounts | Reels | Reels is the only Meta organic format explicitly designed and ranked for discovery beyond a follower graph — its own ranking signal set (above) weights reshare/watch-through likelihood, not follower-graph proximity, more heavily than Feed does [KB-SOCIAL-003]. |
| Deepen relationship with existing followers/customers | Feed photo/carousel, Stories | Feed ranking's top signal group is "your activity" and "your history of interacting with someone" [KB-SOCIAL-001] — it rewards accounts a viewer already engages with; a new-to-them account gets little Feed reach regardless of post quality. |
| Drive a specific reply/DM (review request, booking nudge) | Stories with a sticker (poll/question/link), or a direct Feed post with an explicit ask | Comments and shares are named Feed predictions [KB-SOCIAL-002]; an explicit call-to-comment/reply is a legitimate way to earn that signal — never manufactured (see `community-and-dm-management.md` on engagement-bait bans). |
| A completed-project showcase (a home-services company's equivalent of a case study) | Carousel (multiple photos, before/after) or Reel (short before/after video) | Carousels are not explicitly ranked in the sources above, but Instagram's own historical guidance and this corpus's shared buyer-psychology doctrine agree: a homeowner audience responds to visual proof (before/after), and a carousel or Reel is the only format that shows a sequence, not a single static frame. |

## Posting cadence — no single "right number," reject any doctrine that claims one

No primary source located during this pass states a specific optimal posting frequency (e.g. "post
3x/week") as a ranking factor. Ranking depends on the signal groups above, evaluated per-post — the
number of posts per week is not itself a named ranking input in either official source cited above.
**Reject any recommendation that states a specific weekly posting count as an algorithmic
requirement without citing where that number came from.** See `content-calendar-doctrine.md` for
how a capacity-constrained business should actually decide cadence (capacity- and
content-quality-bound, not algorithm-mandated).

## Contractor bend

A small owner-operated `local_service` business (its own end customer, the homeowner, is described
in `shared/homeowner-buyer-psychology.md`) does not run
a social media department. The practical translation of the ranking doctrine above: post fewer,
better things — a real before/after Reel of a finished bathroom, shot on a phone, gets more reach
per unit of the owner's actual time than a daily grind of generic tips, because Reels ranking rewards
watch-through and reshare likelihood, and a real project photo/video earns both far more reliably
than stock-feeling content does. Never recommend a posting cadence this business cannot sustain
without a dedicated person — a doctrine that assumes a marketing team's bandwidth misfires here
exactly as `shared/contractor-buyer-psychology.md` warns generic SaaS-marketing doctrine will for a
software buyer, and just as badly for a home-services owner-operator with no marketing staff.
