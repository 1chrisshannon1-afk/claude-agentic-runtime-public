# Job Boards and Indeed

## What this file answers

Where to post an open role depending on the kind of work (trades/field, office/admin,
healthcare, tech), the specific mechanics and policies of Indeed (the primary, first board for
almost every small US business), and the other channels worth knowing — ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn,
Craigslist, trade schools, unions, referral programs, and local job boards. It also states what an
agent can safely automate versus what must stay in the owner's own hands, and the approval gates
that sit in front of anything actually going live. This file assumes the post itself is already
written to the standard in `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md` — it does not repeat that content.

This file is company-agnostic. Read "the company" for whichever business is hiring, whether that
is a remodeling contractor, a small SaaS company, or a dental office — channel choice differs by
role type, and that is called out explicitly below rather than assumed.

## 1. Where to post, by role type

**[KB-HPOST-040]** There is no single "best" board for every role — the right channel depends on
where the specific candidate pool actually looks for work. The pattern below is a starting point,
not a substitute for checking what has actually worked for the company's own past hires
(internal doctrine).

### 1.1 Trades and field roles (construction, remodeling, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, field service)

**[KB-HPOST-041]** Indeed first (§2) — it is the highest-volume general board and free by default
for a standard post. Layer in: **Indeed Sponsored** for hard-to-fill trade roles where the free
post isn't generating enough volume (§2.1); **trade-specific referral networks** — the crew's own
network is often the highest-quality source for skilled trade hires, since a peer's recommendation
of a fellow tradesperson's actual on-the-job competence is hard to replicate from a resume alone
(internal doctrine); **local trade schools and vocational/technical programs** — many maintain
active job boards or career-services contacts for graduating cohorts, and are a strong source for
entry-level apprentice/helper roles specifically; **union hiring halls**, where the trade is
unionized in the company's area — a union's own hiring hall or referral system is often the fastest
path to pre-vetted, credentialed labor for that trade; **Craigslist**, still meaningfully used by
trade workers in many local markets despite its dated interface — see §5 for its posting
mechanics and constraints; **local/regional job boards** tied to a specific metro area or industry
association (a regional homebuilders' association job board, a local chamber of commerce listing)
where the company's hiring is genuinely local rather than open to remote/relocating candidates.

### 1.2 Office and administrative roles

**[KB-HPOST-042]** Indeed first, plus **ZipRecruiter** (§3) — both are strong general-purpose
boards for office/admin roles and both distribute broadly with minimal setup; **LinkedIn** (§4) is
worth the manual post for roles with any client-facing, coordination, or career-progression
component, since LinkedIn's audience skews toward candidates actively building a professional
profile; **referral programs** (§6) are consistently one of the highest-quality, lowest-cost
sources for office roles specifically, since an existing employee has direct visibility into both
the work and the person they're referring (internal doctrine).

### 1.3 Healthcare roles (clinical and front-desk)

**[KB-HPOST-043]** Indeed first — healthcare postings are a major, well-established category on
the platform; **specialty/vertical boards** exist for many healthcare professions (nursing-specific
boards, dental-hygienist-specific boards, allied-health boards) and are worth identifying per
profession — this file does not enumerate every vertical healthcare board, since the right one
depends on the specific credential/profession being hired (UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation:
naming a specific vertical healthcare board by name here would be asserting a fact about a board
this pass did not independently verify; identify and verify the right vertical board for the
specific credential before relying on it); **licensing-board or professional-association job
boards** (a state nursing board's career center, a dental hygienists' association job board) are
frequently under-used but high-signal for credentialed roles, since they reach exactly the licensed
population the role requires; **referral programs** are especially strong in healthcare given how
often hiring is credential-gated — an existing licensed staff member's network is pre-filtered for
the license itself.

### 1.4 Tech roles (engineering, product, technical sales)

**[KB-HPOST-044]** Indeed and **LinkedIn** (§4) both matter here, with LinkedIn typically carrying
more weight for tech-specific roles given how much of that candidate pool maintains an active
LinkedIn presence as part of their professional identity; **ZipRecruiter** (§3) is a reasonable
broad-distribution complement; **tech-specific/niche job boards** exist by stack or specialty and
are worth identifying per role (a board specific to a programming language community, a
remote-work-specific board if the role is remote) — again, this file does not name specific
niche tech boards, since board quality and relevance shift too quickly to assert as settled fact
here (UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation: verify current relevance of any specific niche tech
board before relying on it for a real hiring decision); **referral programs** — engineering hires
in particular are frequently sourced through existing engineers' networks, and many small
companies find this their single best channel for technical roles (internal doctrine).

### 1.5 Decision rule for channel selection

- Start every role with Indeed (free tier, §2) regardless of role type — it costs nothing and
  reaches the broadest pool.
- Layer a second broad board (ZipRecruiter or LinkedIn) for roles with weak response after the
  first 1-2 weeks on Indeed alone (internal doctrine — a practice target for when to escalate
  channels, not a number sourced from Indeed or any vendor; adjust to the company's own observed
  response patterns).
- Always activate the company's own referral program (§6) in parallel with any paid channel — it
  is typically free or low-cost and frequently outperforms paid boards on candidate quality.
- Add a vertical/niche channel (trade school, union hall, licensing board, tech community board)
  specifically when the role requires a credential or skill that a general board's keyword search
  does not reliably surface.
- Use Craigslist and other attended-only boards as a supplemental channel for genuinely local
  roles, never as the sole channel, given the manual-posting constraint in §5/§7.

## 2. Indeed specifics

### 2.1 Free vs. sponsored posts

**[KB-HPOST-045]** Indeed allows an employer to post **up to three free jobs per calendar month**,
with each free post remaining visible for **up to 30 days** before it stops appearing in search
(official — Indeed, "Free vs Sponsored Jobs on Indeed,"
`https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/free-vs-sponsored-jobs-on-indeed`, retrieved
2026-09-28). Free posts lose search ranking over time as newer posts (free and sponsored) are
added, which is why a free post's effective visibility window is often shorter in practice than
its full 30-day listed lifespan (official, same source, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-046]** **Sponsored Jobs** run on a budget-based model — the employer sets a budget and
Indeed uses market data to pace the spend against expected application volume for that
role/location; pricing varies by job title, location, and local market conditions, shown upfront
before the employer commits (official, same source, retrieved 2026-09-28). Sponsored posts maintain
prominent placement without needing to be reposted, and a "Premium" sponsored tier appears in the
top three search results **up to 2.0X more often** than the standard sponsored tier in the United
States, per Indeed's own reported data (official, Indeed, "Free vs Sponsored Jobs on Indeed,"
retrieved 2026-09-28 — Indeed's own wording is "up to 2.0X," a ceiling figure, not a typical or
average multiplier).

**[KB-HPOST-047]** **Decision rule:** use the free tier by default for every role. Move to
Sponsored specifically when (a) three free-post slots for the month are already used, (b) a role
is genuinely hard to fill and the free tier's response window (internal doctrine — a practice
target of roughly 1-2 weeks, not an Indeed-published figure) isn't generating enough
qualified volume, or (c) the role is time-sensitive enough that the sponsored tier's budget-paced
guarantee of steady application volume is worth the cost. Sponsoring a role is a real, ongoing
spend decision — treat it the same as any other recurring cost commitment requiring the same
level of sign-off the company uses for other vendor spend (internal doctrine, generalizing the
"paid boards are metered, free boards are not" cost-control pattern from a studied hiring system's
own billing design — the specific dollar figures involved there are not portable and are not
restated here).

### 2.2 Job posting quality and prohibited content

**[KB-HPOST-048]** Indeed enforces its own content-quality and anti-discrimination rules
independent of what US law requires, and will remove, hide, or suspend a post that violates them.
Discrimination-adjacent prohibitions mirror (and in some cases go further than) the EEOC guidance
covered in `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md` §3: no age preference/exclusion language unless
legally required, no gendered job titles or gender/orientation preference, no national-origin or
accent-based exclusion (a language *skill* requirement stated neutrally is fine; a national-origin
proxy is not), no race-based preference, no disability/genetic-information/marital-status/physical-
appearance/socioeconomic-status screens, and no request for a headshot photo except for acting or
modeling roles (official — Indeed, "Discrimination and Hate Offenses,"
`https://www.indeed.com/help/employers/articles/discrimination-and-hate-offenses`, retrieved
2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-049]** Beyond discrimination rules, Indeed's own Job Sync XML feed reference documents
several quality requirements that apply to any posting, not just feed-based ones: postings must
represent **real, currently open positions** — clients must be legitimate companies and the listed
jobs must be open, with outdated or filled jobs removed from a feed promptly, since Indeed can
require an employer to correct the behavior to keep using the feed; job descriptions must be
**complete** (all job-related text, including required qualifications, included even if it lives
elsewhere in the employer's own system); and a job title must not include extra text such as
location, job type, shift, or catchphrases, since Indeed's own examples show a title like "Work with
top clients! 2 days off per week!" as a documented error to avoid. Indeed can exclude a job from
search results for missing required fields or for not meeting Indeed's own quality rules (official —
Indeed Partner Docs, "Job Sync XML feed reference," `https://docs.indeed.com/job-sync-xml/xml-feed`,
retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-050]** **Decision rule:** run every draft post against both the discrimination checklist
(claim 048, and the fuller EEO treatment in `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md` §3) and the
quality checklist (claim 049) before it goes to the approval gate in §7. A post that gets flagged or
removed after going live costs the time-to-fill clock as much as one that was never posted — catching
this before publish is strictly cheaper than a post-hoc fix.

### 2.3 Indeed Apply, screener questions, and the XML job feed

**[KB-HPOST-051]** **Indeed Apply** is Indeed's own in-platform application flow — a candidate
applies without leaving Indeed, submitting a resume (and answering any screener questions) through
Indeed's own interface, which then delivers the application to the employer. It is the standard
apply mechanism for both directly-posted and feed-synced jobs (official — Indeed Partner Docs,
"Indeed Apply," `https://docs.indeed.com/indeed-apply/`, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-052]** **Screener questions** are pre-application questions attached to a posting.
Indeed distinguishes **required** pre-made questions (an applicant who fails a required question is
automatically moved to a Rejected tab, though the employer can review and reverse this manually)
from **preferred** questions (an applicant who misses a preferred criterion stays in the active
pool with a note, rather than being auto-rejected). **Custom, employer-written questions cannot be
marked required** — only Indeed's own pre-made question library supports the required/auto-reject
behavior; a custom question can only ever be "preferred." Indeed recommends 3-5 focused screener
questions to balance filtering value against applicant drop-off (official — Indeed, "How to Use
Screener Questions on Indeed,"
`https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/how-to-use-screener-questions-on-indeed`, retrieved
2026-09-28). For a free post, screener questions are managed from the Jobs page's Edit Job flow
under "Customized pre-screening"; for a Sponsored post, they appear in the flow immediately after
the sponsorship confirmation page (official, same source, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-053]** **The XML job feed** (Indeed calls the modern version "Job Sync") is the
API/feed-based route for an employer or its ATS to publish jobs to Indeed automatically, rather
than posting through Indeed's own web UI job-by-job. A feed is structured as a `<source>` root
element carrying `<publisher>`/`<publisherurl>` identifying fields, with one `<job>` element per
posting. Required or conditionally-required fields per job include: **title** (the position name
only — never location, salary, or job type folded into the title string), **date** (ISO-8601
publish date), a **unique reference number** per job instance, a **requisition ID** that stays
consistent if the same underlying role is reposted, a **URL** to the job's own description page
(not the application page), **company** name, **location** fields (city/state/country, and postal
code if shown on the employer's own career site — a region-wide posting with no single city uses
blank city/postal fields with state and country populated), an **email** contact for account
verification, and a full **description** matching the employer's own career-site content
word-for-word. Conditionally-required fields — required only if the employer's own career site
displays them — include salary, education level, job type, experience requirements, and expiration
date (official — Indeed Partner Docs, "Job Sync XML feed reference,"
`https://docs.indeed.com/job-sync-xml/xml-feed`, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-054]** Indeed Apply is wired into the feed through an `<indeed-apply-data>` block per
job, carrying Indeed-Apply-specific configuration (an API token, the job ID, the application
post-back URL, and any screener-question configuration for that job) so that applications
submitted through Indeed Apply route back to the employer's own system rather than only living
inside Indeed (official, same source as claim 053, retrieved 2026-09-28). **A feed must include
every currently-open job from the employer's own career page** — an incomplete feed risks Indeed
rejecting it outright or limiting the visibility of the jobs it does contain, and a **filled or
closed role must be removed from the feed promptly**, since Indeed's own quality rules treat an
outdated listing as a signal to hide or exclude a job from search (official, same source, retrieved
2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-055]** **Decision rule for feed vs. manual posting:** prefer the XML feed / Job Sync
route whenever the company has (or can set up) an ATS or careers-page system capable of generating
one — it scales to any number of open roles with no marginal per-post effort and is the mechanism
most likely to stay compliant with Indeed's own freshness/completeness rules automatically. Use
manual, one-off posting through Indeed's web UI for a company with only occasional, low-volume
hiring where standing up a feed isn't worth the setup effort.

### 2.4 Applicant retrieval and messaging candidates

**[KB-HPOST-056]** Applicants surface in the employer's **Indeed Employer Dashboard**, which
supports filtering by the screener-question results (required-question failures auto-sorted to a
Rejected tab, per claim 052), additional filters (education, location, experience), a Yes/Maybe/No
tagging system, and a pipeline-status field (Awaiting review, Reviewed, Contacting, Rejected,
Hired) (official — Indeed, "Hiring Guide: How to Use Indeed's Employer Tools,"
`https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/hiring-guide-using-employer-tools`, retrieved
2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-057]** Indeed supports **messaging candidates** directly through the platform,
including saved message templates for personalized outreach at scale; Indeed's own guidance states
that candidates contacted proactively (before they apply) are meaningfully more likely to end up
interviewing, and that employers who respond consistently can earn a "Responsive Employer" badge
that signals reliability to prospective candidates browsing the post (official, same source as
claim 056, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-058]** **Automated retrieval or messaging through anything other than Indeed's own
dashboard and messaging tools (e.g., a browser-automation script that logs into the employer
account and scrapes the applicants list) is a fallback, not a default, and carries real risk** — see
§7's carve-out conditions, `../selection/screening-scoring-and-interviews.md`, and
`../approval-gates-and-pipeline.md` §5 (job-board account reads) for the same fail-closed, scoped,
TTL-expiring-approval pattern, and this file's own §8 for the approval-gates mechanics specific to
job-board actions, before any such automation is built or run. Indeed's own dashboard and messaging
tools are the first-choice mechanism for every routine applicant-management task; browser automation
is reserved for a genuine gap the dashboard itself cannot cover (internal doctrine).

### 2.5 Glassdoor

**[KB-HPOST-059]** As of Indeed and Glassdoor's corporate merger (Glassdoor LLC merged into
Indeed, Inc., completed **2026-07-01**), Glassdoor's job listings are now powered by, and sourced
from, Indeed's job data — a job posted on Indeed appears on Glassdoor automatically, with no
separate Glassdoor posting step required, and distribution runs one-directional (posting attempts
made directly on Glassdoor redirect to Indeed to complete). Glassdoor continues to operate as a
distinct brand for reviews, ratings, salary data, and anonymous workplace discussion, layered on
top of the shared job-listing data (third-party — reporting on the completed merger, e.g. "Glassdoor's
Long Merger With Indeed Is Finally Done," `reworked.co`, retrieved 2026-09-28, corroborated by
Indeed's own consumer-facing page "Glassdoor and Indeed: How Job Seekers Can Leverage Both,"
`indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/glassdoor-and-indeed-for-job-seekers`, retrieved 2026-09-28
— note Indeed's own direct employer-help documentation did not surface an explicit statement of
this mechanic in the specific employer-tools page checked during this pass, so this claim rests on
third-party and consumer-facing sourcing rather than an employer-policy page; confirm against
Indeed's current employer help center before treating "post once, appears on both" as an
unconditional guarantee for a specific account).

**[KB-HPOST-060]** **Decision rule:** do not build or run a separate Glassdoor posting step. Post
once to Indeed and treat Glassdoor visibility as a downstream consequence of that single post,
re-verifying with a quick manual check (does the job actually appear on Glassdoor after posting to
Indeed) rather than assuming claim 059 holds unconditionally for every account type.

## 3. ZipRecruiter

**[KB-HPOST-061]** ZipRecruiter is a broad-distribution job board comparable in role to Indeed —
one posting distributes to a network of partner job sites. It is a reasonable second channel
alongside Indeed for office/admin, healthcare, and general-purpose roles (§1). This file does not
independently verify ZipRecruiter's current pricing or posting-mechanics details — a company or
agent evaluating ZipRecruiter as a channel should pull current pricing and policy directly from
ZipRecruiter's own employer-facing documentation before committing spend, the same discipline

## 4. LinkedIn — manual only

**[KB-HPOST-062]** LinkedIn's own Jobs Terms and Conditions prohibit "any automated means or form
of scraping or data extraction to access, modify, download, query or otherwise collect information"
from LinkedIn's properties, and separately prohibit a customer from copying or reproducing job
listings from a hiring company's own site and uploading them to LinkedIn without that company's
prior knowledge and authorization (official — LinkedIn, "Jobs Terms and Conditions,"
`https://www.linkedin.com/legal/jobs-terms-conditions`, retrieved 2026-09-28). LinkedIn's broader
User Agreement additionally prohibits developing or using bots, scripts, or other automated methods
to access LinkedIn's services generally (official — LinkedIn Help, "Prohibited software and
extensions," `https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387`, retrieved 2026-09-28).

**[KB-HPOST-063]** **Decision rule: LinkedIn job posting is manual-only, done by the owner or an
authorized human in the browser — never automated (no scripted posting, no scripted applicant
retrieval, no automated messaging through LinkedIn).** LinkedIn does offer legitimate partner APIs
(a documented Jobs API / partner integration path) for employers who want programmatic posting —
using that documented, authorized partner path is a different thing from scraping the consumer UI,
and is out of scope for this file's default assumption that a small business has no such partner
relationship in place; if a company does have a genuine LinkedIn partner API relationship, treat it
like any other documented-API channel (prefer it over browser automation, per the job-board
carve-out doctrine referenced in §7) (internal doctrine, applying the general "documented API is
preferred over scraping" principle from the job-board carve-out doctrine referenced in §7 to this
specific platform's stated ToS).

## 5. Craigslist

**[KB-HPOST-064]** Craigslist's own Terms of Use prohibit automated posting: "you agree not to
copy/collect CL content via robots, spiders, scripts, scrapers, crawlers, or any automated or
manual equivalent," and separately require that anyone posting on behalf of a business have actual
authority to do so (official — Craigslist, "Terms of Use,"
`https://www.craigslist.org/about/terms.of.use/en`, retrieved 2026-09-28). Craigslist's jobs
category is also rate-limited by convention/policy to roughly one post per position per local area
within a recurring window (commonly cited as around 30 days), and near-duplicate postings within a
short window (commonly cited as 48 hours) or across multiple regional sites/categories
simultaneously are against Craigslist's posting norms (third-party — multiple posting-service
summaries of Craigslist's practical posting-cadence rules, retrieved 2026-09-28; Craigslist's own
Terms of Use page incorporates a separate "prohibited content"/posting-flag list by reference that
was not independently read in full during this pass — treat the specific cadence numbers as
UNVERIFIED — seeded, needs derivation until confirmed against Craigslist's own current posting
rules for the specific local site in question).

**[KB-HPOST-065]** **Decision rule: Craigslist posting is attended-browser-only, done by a human,
never headless-automated** — this follows directly from Craigslist's own stated ban on automated
posting (claim 064) and is the same posture the job-board carve-out doctrine in §7 requires for any
board with no documented API. Craigslist remains a genuinely useful channel for local trade and
service roles (§1.1) despite the manual-only constraint — the constraint affects *how* it gets
posted, not whether it's worth posting to.

## 6. Trade schools, unions, and referral programs

**[KB-HPOST-066]** **Trade schools / vocational programs.** Most maintain a career-services contact
or job board for their graduating cohorts. This is a relationship worth establishing once (a
standing point of contact) rather than a one-off posting transaction — a company that hires
apprentices or entry-level trade staff repeatedly benefits from an ongoing relationship with a
specific program's career-services office more than from a cold one-time posting (internal
doctrine).

**[KB-HPOST-067]** **Union hiring halls.** Where a trade is unionized in the company's area, the
union's own hiring hall or referral system is often the single fastest and best-vetted labor
source for that trade — credentialing and basic competency screening are frequently handled
upstream by the union itself. This channel is entirely relationship- and jurisdiction-specific;
this file does not name a specific union or hall, since the correct one depends entirely on the
trade and the company's actual location (internal doctrine).

**[KB-HPOST-068]** **Referral programs.** An internal employee-referral program — a standing
incentive (a bonus paid on a successful hire and typically on that hire completing some minimum
tenure) for current staff who refer a new hire — is consistently one of the lowest-cost,
highest-quality channels across every role type in §1, because an existing employee has real,
first-hand knowledge of both the work and the person they're vouching for. **Decision rule:** run a
referral program in parallel with every paid or unpaid board posting, not as a fallback used only
when boards underperform — the two are complementary, not sequential (internal doctrine).

**[KB-HPOST-069]** **Local job boards.** A local chamber of commerce, a regional industry
association, or a city/metro-specific job board can meaningfully outperform a national board for a
role that is genuinely local and not open to remote or relocating candidates, since the audience is
pre-filtered by geography. Identify these per the company's actual metro area — this file does not
name specific local boards, since they are inherently local and not something a company-agnostic
KB can enumerate (internal doctrine).

## 7. What can be automated safely vs. what must be done by the owner in the browser

**[KB-HPOST-070]** **Prefer a documented API, XML feed, or first-party export over browser
automation, always.** This is the single governing principle for every channel in this file: where
a board offers a documented, sanctioned integration path (Indeed's XML/Job Sync feed, a genuine
LinkedIn partner API, a documented ATS integration), use it. Browser automation against a board's
consumer web UI is the last-resort tier, reserved for boards with no documented API at all — and
even then, it must be built with the same hardening any credential-bearing automation needs:
execution that never puts the login credential or scraped candidate PII into an LLM-visible or
logged channel, an account-identity check before every session (never trust-on-first-use — verify
the logged-in account is actually the company's own board account, not a different business's),
anti-bot/CAPTCHA detection that hard-stops rather than retries, and rate limiting tuned conservatively
against the target site's real observed limits rather than pushed as fast as technically possible
(internal doctrine — this is a general job-board automation safety pattern, not specific to any one
platform; it applies with equal force to Craigslist, an un-partnered LinkedIn integration, or any
future board with no documented API).

**[KB-HPOST-071]** **What can reasonably be automated (drafting and preparation only):**
- Drafting the job post text itself (title, summary, duties, requirements, pay, benefits, schedule,
  location, how-to-apply) per `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md`.
- Generating or updating an XML feed entry for Indeed, once the feed mechanism itself is already
  set up and the posting has been approved (§8).
- Filtering and sorting applicants that have already been retrieved through Indeed's own dashboard
  or an equivalent first-party channel (never scraping the applicant list itself, absent the
  hardening in claim 070).
- Drafting outreach/messaging templates for the owner to review and send, or sending through a
  first-party messaging API/integration the company has actually authorized.
- Tracking pipeline status, screening notes, and scorecards in whatever system the company already
  uses for this.

**[KB-HPOST-072]** **What must stay with the owner, done by hand in the browser (or by explicit,
logged approval before an agent acts):**
- Actually publishing any post to any board — nothing goes live without the approval gate in §8.
- Posting to any board with no documented API (Craigslist, an un-partnered LinkedIn posting) —
  manual, attended, in the owner's own browser session, per §4-§5.
- Any board-account login on a channel where the company has not already set up and authorized a
  credentialed automation path.
- Sending any outbound message to a real candidate — draft it, never send it, without the owner's
  explicit go-ahead (see `../approval-gates-and-pipeline.md` §1, gate 2 — "no outbound message of
  any kind ... is sent without approval").
- Retrieving applicant data through any mechanism other than Indeed's own dashboard/API, unless a
  scoped, time-limited, re-verified-at-execution approval has been explicitly granted for that
  specific retrieval (the same TTL-expiring, fail-closed pattern is the reference design for any
  such approval, regardless of which board it's built for) (internal doctrine).

## 8. Approval gates before anything goes live

**[KB-HPOST-073]** **Nothing described in this file is ever auto-published.** Every job posting
follows a draft-then-approve flow: a draft is prepared (title through how-to-apply, checked against
both `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md` and this file's board-specific quality/discrimination
rules), and a specific, explicit approval step — a real human decision, not a default timeout —
gates it actually going live on any board. This applies identically whether the channel is the free
Indeed tier, a paid Sponsored post, an XML feed entry, or a manual Craigslist post (internal
doctrine, generalizing the draft→decision→publish pattern from a studied hiring workflow's own
design, restated here without naming that system).

**[KB-HPOST-074]** **Decision rule — gates that must exist before anything is treated as "done":**

1. **Before a post goes live on any board:** an explicit approval, reviewing the actual drafted
   text (not a summary of it), against both the EEO/pay-wording rules in
   `role-requirements-and-job-posts.md` and the board-specific rules in this file.
2. **Before any outbound message reaches a real candidate** (an interview invite, a decline, an
   offer, a cold-outreach message): explicit approval of that specific message's content — never a
   standing blanket approval that lets future messages skip review.
3. **Before any browser-automation retrieval or posting action runs against a board with no
   documented API:** a scoped approval naming exactly which board, which job/account, and — where
   retrieval is involved — a real expiration on that approval, re-checked at the moment the
   automation actually runs, not only at the moment it was granted.
4. **Before spending money on any paid board tier** (Sponsored posts, a paid ZipRecruiter/LinkedIn
   tier): treat this like any other recurring or one-off vendor spend decision requiring the
   company's normal spend sign-off — never something an agent commits the company to
   unilaterally.

**[KB-HPOST-075]** A single, knowledgeable approver is sufficient for routine hiring actions in a
small business context — this does not need a two-person sign-off process by default, though a
company that already runs a stricter internal approval policy for other business decisions should
apply that same policy here rather than treating hiring as a carve-out exception (internal
doctrine).

### 8.1 Decision rules (summary)

- Draft first, always — never generate a live posting or send a live message as a first action.
- Every board-going-live action needs its own explicit approval; approving one post does not
  pre-approve the next one.
- Any automation against a board without a documented API needs a scoped, time-limited, explicitly
  re-verified approval — never a standing grant.
- Any spend commitment (a sponsored post, a paid board tier) goes through the same sign-off the
  company already uses for other spend decisions.

### 8.2 Anti-patterns

- Auto-publishing a drafted post because it "looks fine" and the deadline is tight.
- Treating one board-account login or one approved retrieval as a green light to keep pulling
  applicant data indefinitely without re-approval.
- Scraping a board's consumer UI when a documented API or feed already exists for that need.
- Sending a real candidate a message drafted by an agent without a human reading the actual final
  text first.
- Committing the company to a paid posting tier without the same spend sign-off any other vendor
  cost would require.
