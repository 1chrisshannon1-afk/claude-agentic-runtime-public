# LinkedIn Native Video & Build-in-Public Founder Content — B2B Small-Business Software

Terse, worker-applicable rules for LinkedIn native video and founder build-in-public content
targeting B2B small-business software buyers. Read the specific buyer persona from the company
pack's `persona_file` or that company's dossier — never assume one; the contractor/trade-buyer
guidance lives only in the conditional "Contractor/trade-buyer bend" section below. Figures are
aggregated from third-party creator-tool guides and benchmark reports current as of retrieval date
2026-09-12 unless marked OFFICIAL (platform-published) or UNCITED (practitioner consensus, no
single authoritative source). LinkedIn publishes almost none of its ranking internals — treat
"algorithm" claims below as third-party inference, not confirmed platform statement, unless marked
OFFICIAL.

## Hard constraints (cross-company operator rulings — never contradict, regardless of what a source below says)

- B-roll ≤2.5 s per shot; ≤20% of total runtime; never two b-roll shots consecutively; none before
  the 3.0 s mark.
- Every cut opens on the product itself (product-first opens) — not a face, not a logo, not scenery.
- Vocabulary and claims are per company, not universal: banned terms, numeric-claim restrictions,
  and any other wording rule: follow the company pack's `claims_policy` and that company's own
  video-producer dossier; where neither states them, DEGRADED — never invent, assume, or carry over another company's list
  (see `INDEX.md`'s precedence note).
- Viewing review: the rendered cut is watched — contact sheets against the timed transcript, per shot (primer §4/§7) — before any upload; the seat's own frame-viewing review satisfies this, no separate human viewer is required. No upload skips this step.

## Format choice — video is not always the right call

- Native document posts (PDF carousels) currently out-engage every other format: ~7.00% average
  engagement vs. ~6.00–6.60% for native video, ~39% more reach and ~30% more engagement than the
  platform average; carousels beat video by ~45% and plain text by ~125%. Video reach was reported
  down ~36% YoY into 2026.
  [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin),
  [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-document-posts-on-linkedin-see-more-engagement/816551/),
  [Meet Lea](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-carousel-engagement-statistics)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator benchmarks — no official LinkedIn breakdown exists; treat as directional)
- Don't default to video because it "feels native." Use a document/carousel for lists, before/afters,
  steps, or stats; reserve video for content needing motion, voice, or a live screen.
- Screen recordings of real features in use are reported as the highest-engaging founder video
  subtype for SaaS — beats staged demos and animated explainers for this audience.
  [jonathanrintala.com](https://jonathanrintala.com/blog/build-in-public-saas-top-founders-to-follow/)
  (UNCITED — practitioner consensus from build-in-public case studies)
- Video's comparative edge: 93% of B2B buyers report video builds brand trust, 91% report more
  purchase confidence after watching — video shows a real person and real software, which a
  carousel can't.
  [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/10-best-saas-product-demo-video-examples-for-2026-with-real-examples-you-can-watch)
  (UNCITED — original survey source not independently verified)

## Length

- Under 30 s: highest completion rate (reported up to ~200% higher than longer video) and best for
  cold/discovery reach.
  [Puritano](https://www.puritano.com/post/linkedin-video-best-practices),
  [Ligo Social](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-video-post-best-practices)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator benchmarking)
- 30–90 s: general sweet spot for founder posts aimed at people who don't yet follow you.
  [Visla](https://www.visla.us/blog/guides/linkedin-video-in-2026-whats-working-and-how-to-make-it/)
- 2–5 min: only for warm audiences who already know the founder/brand (thought-leadership, fuller
  walkthrough, AMA answer) — do not open cold with this length.
  [Shootsta](https://shootsta.com/blog/linkedin-video-length-guide)
- Platform ceiling: 10 min mobile upload, 15 min desktop — a ceiling, not a target.
  [Sendspark](https://blog.sendspark.com/linkedin-video-specs)
  (OFFICIAL-adjacent: cites LinkedIn's published upload limits; platform doesn't publish "best
  length" guidance)
- Suggested default (a company dossier may override): 30–60 s for new-feature/build-in-public clips; go longer only for a
  full-walkthrough to an audience that already follows the account.

## Sound-off design (mandatory, not optional)

- LinkedIn autoplays video muted in-feed (organic and sponsored); ~85% of social video overall is
  watched without sound.
  [Blue Gift Digital](https://bluegiftdigital.com/what-linkedin-video-autoplay-creators-2025/),
  [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/linkedin-video-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- Design for 100% comprehension with sound off: burned-in captions PLUS on-screen text/graphics
  carrying the key point — captions alone are not enough.
- Caption formatting: 1–2 lines/frame, ~42 characters/line, 2–4 s on screen per card.
  [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/linkedin-video-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- Follow WCAG 2.1 AA for caption accuracy, timing, and contrast — an accessibility requirement, not
  just an engagement tactic.
  [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/linkedin-video-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- Don't ship LinkedIn's raw auto-captions as final — accuracy is inconsistent enough that creators
  routinely replace them; burn in reviewed captions.
  [Tomorrow Communications](https://www.tomorrowcommunications.com/blog/Remove-LinkedIn-Auto-Captions)
  (UNCITED — single practitioner source)
- The exported first frame must explain the topic alone, with zero motion/audio — many viewers see
  a static frame before playback starts.
  [Hera](https://hera.video/blog/create-linkedin-product-videos-with-ai-motion-design)
  (UNCITED — practitioner consensus)

## Canvas / aspect ratio

- Square (1:1, 1080×1080): safest default — more vertical feed space than 16:9 on desktop and
  mobile, no letterboxing.
  [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ideal-image-video-sizes-linkedin-posts/)
- Vertical 4:5 (1080×1350): optimal for talking-head/founder clips and short tips — fills the phone
  screen nearly edge to edge, reported ~25% better mobile dwell than 1:1.
  [Post Everywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/linkedin-aspect-ratios)
  (UNCITED — no official source for the specific percentage)
- Full vertical 9:16 (1080×1920): standard for LinkedIn's dedicated short-form vertical feed
  (TikTok-style scroll, fully live 2026) — use only when targeting that surface specifically.
  [Quso.ai](https://quso.ai/blog/linkedin-video-specs)
- Suggested default (a company dossier may override): 1:1 or 4:5 for standard feed posts; 9:16 only as an explicit
  short-form-vertical-feed leg. Never submit 16:9 landscape as the primary in-feed export — it
  under-fills mobile and reads as a repurposed YouTube asset.

## Native upload vs. external link (never link out in the body)

- Native-uploaded video reach is reported as high as 5x an equivalent external link post.
  [Sendspark](https://blog.sendspark.com/how-to-send-videos-on-linkedin)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator benchmarking — no official multiplier published)
- Any external link in the post body (YouTube included) is reported to cut distribution ~30–60%
  versus a link-free native post.
  [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2026/07/30/the-linkedin-link-penalty-cutting-your-reach-by-60/)
- The "link in first comment" workaround is reported largely patched — 2026 systems reportedly
  detect comment-funnel patterns and penalize similarly.
  [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2026/07/30/the-linkedin-link-penalty-cutting-your-reach-by-60/)
  (UNCITED beyond one journalistic aggregation — treat "detection" mechanics as unconfirmed)
- Rule: always native-upload video directly. Never post a YouTube/Vimeo link as the primary post;
  if a link must exist, accept the reach cost or rely on profile/bio links instead.

## First-line hook / "see more" mechanics

- Post text folds after ~140–210 characters (device-dependent) before "…see more" — the pre-fold
  text is the entire hook.
  [LinkedIn Post Preview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-hook-formulas-2026)
  (UNCITED — no official character-count publication)
- Keep the hook to 1–2 short sentences whose only job is earning the click — don't summarize the
  whole post above the fold.
  [Magicpost](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-hooks)
- "See more" click-through is a reported first-~60-minute distribution signal, but high clicks with
  low dwell afterward is reported to be penalized — an over-promising hook hurts reach.
  [LinkGenie](https://linkgenie.one/blog/how-the-linkedin-algorithim-actually-works)
  (UNCITED — practitioner inference, not an official statement)
- For video, the hook text and the video's first 1–3 seconds must make the same promise — write the
  caption to match the opening shot, never a mismatched teaser.
- The first video frame must independently make sense with zero text-post context.

## Video hook (first 1–3 seconds)

- The single highest-leverage lever for video performance is reported to be a hook in the first
  1–3 seconds plus captions from frame one.
  [Puritano](https://www.puritano.com/post/linkedin-video-best-practices)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator claim)
- Open on the real product per the hard constraint above — this also satisfies the "hook in 1–3
  seconds" requirement: a real screen, a real result, a real number the buyer cares about.
- Never open with slow build-up, a logo animation, or "Hi, I'm [name]…" — widely flagged as the top
  reason a cold viewer scrolls past in under 3 seconds.
  [Medium](https://medium.com/@viralboris/linkedin-hooks-that-actually-work-in-2026-50-examples-bbe7976cde67)
  (UNCITED — practitioner consensus)

## Build-in-public content substance (what to actually show)

- Founder personal-profile content is reported to consistently outperform company-page content for
  B2B SaaS — post from the founder's account as primary; treat the company page as a secondary
  amplifier.
  [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/the-b2b-linkedin-strategy-guide)
- What performs, reported highest to lower: screen recordings of real working features; transparent
  revenue/metrics updates (wins AND setbacks both build trust — "hit $X" and "churn spiked" alike);
  real customer stories/testimonials; lessons-learned narrative; behind-the-scenes process.
  [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-content-ideas-for-b2b-saas-founders-100-post-ideas-to-build-pipeline),
  [jonathanrintala.com](https://jonathanrintala.com/blog/build-in-public-saas-top-founders-to-follow/)
- Radical transparency (setbacks, not just wins) is repeatedly cited as the trust mechanism that
  separates build-in-public from ordinary marketing — small-business buyers respond to authenticity
  over a highlight reel.
  [vibrantsnap](https://www.vibrantsnap.com/blog/build-in-public-complete-guide-founders)
  (UNCITED — practitioner consensus)
- Effective structure: open on a real problem/question, show the product solving it, close on a
  concrete outcome or a direct ask (a comment-inviting question) — not a hard sales CTA.
  [Shootsta](https://shootsta.com/blog/product-launch-video-ideas)
- Asking genuinely for feedback (not performatively) is reported to increase engagement — frame
  new-feature clips as "here's what we shipped, tell me what's wrong with it," not a polished
  announcement.
  [Wondershare](https://democreator.wondershare.com/video-presentation/video-linkedin.html)
  (UNCITED — practitioner consensus)
- Small-business buyers specifically are reported skeptical of feature lists — they want
  evidence a tool solves their exact problem with less friction; show a workflow they recognize (a
  real task from their day, a real customer-facing screen), not an abstract capability slide.
  [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/9-amazing-video-ideas-for-saas-in-2026-with-real-examples-you-can-watch)
  (UNCITED — aggregator synthesis)
- 77% of B2B buyers reportedly trust peer reviews/UGC over brand-authored material — weight toward
  real customer moments/quotes over founder-only polish where real material exists.
  [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/10-best-saas-product-demo-video-examples-for-2026-with-real-examples-you-can-watch)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator citation)

## Cadence

- Reported sweet spot: 3–4 posts/week sustained consistently, 4–5/week cited as optimal for
  actively-growing founders; 2/week is a floor below which compounding weakens sharply.
  [Postiv](https://postiv.ai/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-linkedin),
  [MeetEdgar](https://meetedgar.com/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-linkedin)
  (UNCITED beyond aggregator synthesis — no official cadence guidance)
- Consistency of rhythm matters more than any single week's volume — sporadic bursts then silence
  underperform steady lower cadence.
  [Full Throttle Media](https://www.fullthrottlemedia.com/2026/05/why-posting-more-on-linkedin-stopped.html)
- Not every post needs to be video — mix with document/carousel and text per Format choice above;
  a realistic mix for a small founder team is 1–2 video posts inside a 3–4 post/week cadence.
- Sustain for months, not weeks — sources frame founder LinkedIn growth as a 12-month compounding
  effort, not a campaign with an end date.
  [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/the-b2b-linkedin-strategy-guide)

## First-hour ("golden hour") mechanics

- LinkedIn has not officially confirmed a "golden hour," but third-party analysis consistently
  describes the first ~60–90 minutes as a test-distribution window: the post goes to a small slice
  of the network first (reportedly ~2–5%), and early engagement quality decides whether it widens.
  [ContentIn](https://contentin.io/glossary/golden-hour/),
  [GetCrisp](https://getcrisp.co.uk/do-you-know-about-the-golden-hour-of-linkedin/)
  (UNCITED — LinkedIn has not confirmed this mechanism; well-corroborated inference, not fact)
- Comments reportedly now outweigh likes as a ranking input (some sources cite ~2x a like's weight);
  comment quality (real sentences, relevant accounts) matters more than count — generic one-word
  comments or pod activity is reported down-weighted/flagged.
  [Medium/HypergrowthAI](https://medium.com/@HypergrowthAI/5-steps-to-10x-your-linkedin-reach-in-2026-comments-now-count-2x-more-than-likes-aa6d5bedf60d)
  (UNCITED — no official weighting disclosure; treat "2x" as directional only)
- Who comments matters, not just how many — engagement from the target audience (the company's
  buyer persona) reportedly reinforces relevance; unrelated-account engagement can blur it.
  [ContentIn](https://contentin.io/glossary/golden-hour/)
- Publish when the founder can be present for the next 60–90 minutes to reply substantively to every
  early comment — reported to meaningfully extend the test-distribution window.
  [Louise Brogan](https://louisebrogan.com/linkedin-comments/)
- Dwell time (attention held in-feed, separate from clicks/reactions) is described as an
  increasingly weighted "depth score" signal — posts holding attention 5+ seconds reportedly get
  materially more reach than posts scrolled past quickly.
  [Meet Lea](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained)
  (UNCITED — third-party framework, not an official disclosure)
- For video, completion rate and rewatch behavior reportedly carry real algorithmic weight —
  reinforces the length guidance: a short, tightly-edited clip that finishes beats a long one that
  gets abandoned.
  [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)

## Applying the hard constraints to this content type

- Product-first opens and the "hook in 1–3 seconds" finding are the same rule from two directions
  (platform performance and operator ruling) — no tension to resolve: always open on the real
  product/screen.
- The b-roll caps apply on top of a short 30–60 s edit: in a 30 s video the ≤20%-runtime cap is
  roughly 6 s total b-roll, meaning most of the runtime must already be product/screen or
  talking-head content — this aligns with the screen-recording-performs-best finding above.
- The company's banned-term list (pack `claims_policy` or dossier) applies to every text layer of a
  video post — hook line, post body, on-screen captions, voiceover — not narration alone.
- Where the company's claims list forbids numeric time/accuracy claims, that applies to the caption
  text as much as the video — a hook like "cuts <task> time by X%" is then prohibited even though
  platform advice elsewhere favors specific numbers in hooks.
- The viewing review (primer §4/§7 — the rendered cut watched per shot against the timed
  transcript; the seat's own frame-viewing review satisfies it) must happen before native upload —
  LinkedIn gives no meaningful post-publish
  edit path for video (captions/thumbnail aside), so a mistake ships live during the algorithm's
  first-hour test window before it can be caught.

## Contractor/trade-buyer bend (apply only when the company's persona is a contractor or trade buyer)

- Contractor and trade buyers are reported to want plain workflow evidence (a real job, a real
  customer-facing screen), and jargon works against that. Keep every text layer in the buyer's
  own plain words, within the company's `claims_policy`.
  [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/9-amazing-video-ideas-for-saas-in-2026-with-real-examples-you-can-watch)
  (UNCITED — aggregator synthesis)

## Practical checklist for a worker agent producing a LinkedIn founder video post

1. Confirm video is the right format — if it's a list, stat, or before/after, use a document
   carousel instead.
2. Script the first 1–3 seconds to open directly on the real product/screen — no logo, no
   face-to-camera preamble.
3. Cut to 30–60 s for cold/discovery; only exceed this for a warm-audience deep-dive.
4. Export square (1:1) or vertical 4:5 as the primary in-feed asset; 9:16 only for the dedicated
   short-form vertical feed.
5. Burn in reviewed captions (1–2 lines, ~42 characters/line, 2–4 s/card) — never ship raw
   auto-captions.
6. Confirm the exported first frame reads clearly with zero motion/audio.
7. Write the caption hook (1–2 sentences, pre-fold) to match the video's opening shot exactly.
8. Scan every text layer against the company's vocabulary/claims list; remove anything it bans.
9. Check b-roll against all four hard-constraint caps before final export.
10. Run the viewing review (primer §4/§7); do not upload until cleared.
11. Upload natively — never post an external video link as the primary post.
12. Schedule/publish only when the founder can actively reply to comments for the next 60–90
    minutes.
13. Slot into the week's 3–4-post cadence; don't make every post that week video.
